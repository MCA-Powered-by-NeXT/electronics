Expanded Guide: Week 5 Day 4

Troubleshooting practical checkoff, evidence standards, repair-ticket review, and instructor scoring guidance.

Week 5Student Day PageAssessment

CETa Objective Connection

This checkoff verifies CETa-related technician procedures: safe setup, symptom analysis, schematic use, DMM/continuity planning, fault isolation, correction, retest, and documentation. Students must demonstrate process, not just final repair.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Before the checkoff, remind students that a practical assessment measures repeatable technician behavior. A student who randomly finds the fault without a test plan has not demonstrated professional troubleshooting. Score the process: safety, communication, method, evidence, correction, verification, and documentation.

Use controlled low-voltage boards only. If a student creates an unsafe condition, pause the checkoff, correct the safety issue, and record remediation needed.

Student-Facing Expectations

Students receive a fault scenario and must work through it using the troubleshooting method. They must explain the symptom, expected operation, test plan, selected meter setup, findings, correction, and verification. The instructor may ask questions at any point to confirm reasoning.

Instructor Prompt Bank

  • Describe the symptom without naming the cause.
  • What should this circuit do when working correctly?
  • What is your first test and why?
  • What meter setting and lead placement will you use?
  • What result would confirm or reject your hypothesis?
  • What evidence supports your correction?
  • How did you verify the repair?

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Student fixes the circuit before documenting evidence: require a restart or score documentation/process accordingly.
  • Student uses the wrong meter setting: pause and remediate meter safety.
  • Student cannot explain expected operation: return to schematic tracing before checkoff continuation.
  • Student changes multiple items: require one correction at a time and retest.

Documentation Expectations

The repair ticket must include student name, station/board ID, date, symptom, expected operation, safety status, meter setup, test plan, test results, confirmed fault, correction, verification, and instructor signoff.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why does the checkoff score the process and not just the final result?
  2. What information must appear in a repair ticket?
  3. Why is expected operation important before testing?
  4. What should happen if a meter is set up incorrectly?

Exit Ticket

Students identify one troubleshooting habit they performed well and one habit they need to improve before the final practical.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for safety/review, 90 minutes for rotating checkoff stations, 25 minutes for repair-ticket review, and remaining time for coaching, makeups, shutdown, and AAR. Students who do not pass should receive a specific remediation task such as meter setup practice, schematic tracing, or repair-ticket rewrite.
