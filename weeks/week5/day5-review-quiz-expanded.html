CETa Objective Connection

This milestone consolidates CETa readiness for wiring, connectors, technician workmanship, troubleshooting method, DMM/continuity evidence, repair documentation, and practical checkoff performance. No new instruction should be introduced during this session.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Use this day to verify readiness and close gaps. Students should complete the Week 5 quiz, finish or retake troubleshooting checkoffs as needed, correct documentation weaknesses, complete lab makeups, and participate in an AAR. Keep the tone focused on evidence: what did the student demonstrate, what remains weak, and what should be practiced before Week 6 final project work.

Review Topics

  • Difference between symptom, fault, cause, correction, and verification.
  • Why source/path/load thinking matters in troubleshooting.
  • How to choose safe test points.
  • Why continuity testing requires power removed.
  • How workmanship affects reliability and serviceability.
  • What makes a repair ticket complete and professional.

Guided Review Questions

  1. A circuit works only when a wire is wiggled. What type of fault is likely and what evidence would support it?
  2. An LED does not light. What are three checks before replacing the LED?
  3. Why should a technician document the original symptom before making corrections?
  4. What is the danger of changing multiple things before retesting?
  5. How can poor workmanship create future troubleshooting problems?

Hands-On Readiness Task

Students complete a short timed station: inspect a small low-voltage training circuit, identify one workmanship issue, choose one safe test point, write a predicted reading or observation, and complete a short technician note. This is not new instruction; it is a readiness verification task.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may try to memorize quiz answers instead of explaining process. Ask them to justify each answer with a technician reason.
  • Students may write incomplete repair tickets. Require all sections before signoff.
  • Students may forget safety boundaries during makeups. Revisit power-off and meter-mode habits.
  • Students may underestimate workmanship. Show how messy wiring slows troubleshooting.

Documentation Expectations

Collect quiz results, updated repair tickets, make-up checkoff evidence, readiness notes, and AAR responses. These artifacts help determine who needs Week 6 coaching before the final practical and readiness assessment.

Exit Ticket

Students answer: “Before the final project, the troubleshooting skill I am strongest in is _____. The troubleshooting skill I need to practice is _____. My plan to improve is _____.”

Pacing and Remediation

Use 10 minutes for check-in, 10 minutes for safety brief, 35 minutes for quiz, 45 minutes for checkoffs/makeups, 30 minutes for repair-ticket review, 25 minutes for readiness station, and remaining time for AAR/shutdown. Assign targeted remediation for students who struggle with meter setup, schematic tracing, evidence-based conclusions, or documentation.
