CETa Objective Connection

This milestone day supports CETa readiness by checking whether students can explain Week 1 safety concepts, use professional technician language, recognize hazards, describe safe low-voltage boundaries, and document evidence. No new instruction occurs on milestone days; the purpose is review, evidence, coaching, and readiness.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Run this as a performance checkpoint, not a normal lecture. Students should demonstrate what they learned through quiz responses, oral explanation, documentation review, and practical observation. Keep the environment supportive but firm: students who cannot explain the safety loop are not ready for unsupervised circuit work.

Expanded Review Content

Students should be able to explain the Week 1 content without reading directly from notes. They should define a hazard, describe PPE expectations, explain why low-voltage DC training still requires safety practices, identify the parts of a simple circuit, distinguish open circuits from short circuits, and describe why documentation matters.

Use short oral prompts. Example: “You find a damaged jumper lead before lab. What do you do?” The expected answer should include stop, remove from service, notify the instructor, document the condition, and use an approved replacement.

Common Mistakes and Remediation

  • If a student says “be careful” without naming a specific action, ask what they will inspect, disconnect, verify, or document.
  • If a student confuses open and short circuits, return to diagrams and have them trace current path.
  • If a student skips PPE or shutdown language, connect it to course eligibility and lab participation requirements.
  • If quiz scores are low, assign targeted review rather than repeating the entire week.

Documentation Expectations

End the day with a complete Week 1 evidence packet: quiz score, lab signoff, hazard table, simulation log, circuit-path exit ticket, and AAR note. This shows the student is ready to move into Week 2 electrical quantities.

Check for Understanding

  1. What are the steps in the technician safety loop?
  2. What is one example of a hazard that must be escalated?
  3. What makes a circuit complete?
  4. Why is milestone day not used for new instruction?

Exit Ticket

Students write: “Before I begin Week 2 measurements, the safety habit I must use every time is ___ because ___.”

Instructor Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for check-in/safety brief, 25 minutes for review, 30 minutes for quiz, 45 minutes for stations, 30 minutes for makeups/remediation, 20 minutes for inventory/shutdown, and 15 minutes for AAR. Students who do not meet readiness expectations should receive a targeted Week 1 remediation task before Week 2 lab measurement activities.
