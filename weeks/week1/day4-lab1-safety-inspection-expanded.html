CETa Objective Connection

This lab supports CETa readiness in safety precautions, PPE use, workplace procedures, tool inspection, documentation, and technician accountability. Students move from talking about safety to producing observable safety evidence.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Run this day like a real lab entry procedure. Students should not begin lab tasks until PPE, bench condition, tool condition, and power state are verified. This creates the course norm that safety is checked before work, not after a problem.

Correct vague language. A student saying “this bench is messy” should be coached to say, “Loose component leads and unused jumpers are present in the work area and should be removed before lab begins.”

Expanded Student Explanation

A safety inspection is a technician skill. In many workplaces, technicians are expected to inspect tools, work areas, and equipment before starting a task. The inspection protects people, prevents equipment damage, and creates documentation that the work began under controlled conditions.

During this lab, students practice identifying hazards, correcting simple issues, escalating issues that require instructor approval, and signing off only when evidence supports the decision.

Instructor Demonstration Sequence

  1. Model entering the lab and stopping at check-in.
  2. Inspect PPE: glasses, shoes, loose clothing, and assigned safety items.
  3. Inspect tools: wire strippers, cutters, jumpers, meter leads, and storage condition.
  4. Inspect bench: clutter, liquids, loose metal, damaged wires, and power state.
  5. Show a correct documentation entry and an unacceptable vague entry.
  6. Demonstrate end-of-lab shutdown and tool return.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may check boxes without actually inspecting. Require them to point to the item checked.
  • Students may hide damaged tools to avoid delay. Reinforce that reporting damage is professional, not negative.
  • Students may correct a serious hazard without permission. Define what students can correct and what must be escalated.
  • Students may forget shutdown. Treat shutdown as part of the lab grade, not cleanup after the lab.

Documentation Expectations

Each student or team submits a completed safety inspection checklist, a tool condition note, one hazard/corrective-action entry, and a shutdown confirmation. The documentation should show what was found, what was corrected, and who verified it.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why is PPE checked before lab starts?
  2. What is one condition that requires instructor escalation?
  3. Why should damaged tools be removed from service?
  4. What evidence proves a workstation was shut down correctly?

Exit Ticket

Students write one professional hazard note using this format: “I observed ___ at ___; the risk is ___; the corrective action is ___; instructor approval is/is not required.”

Instructor Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for check-in and safety brief, 20 minutes for demonstration, 60 minutes for team inspection work, 20 minutes for documentation coaching, 20 minutes for signoffs, and the remaining time for shutdown/AAR. Students who rush the checklist should repeat inspection with the instructor observing.
