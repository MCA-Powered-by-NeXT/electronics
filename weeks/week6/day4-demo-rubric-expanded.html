CETa Objective Connection

This day connects technical performance to workforce communication. Students demonstrate safe operation, explain circuit function, show measurement evidence, answer troubleshooting questions, and present documentation clearly. CETa readiness includes both knowing the work and communicating the work.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Make the demonstration professional but supportive. Students should present their project as if reporting to a lead technician or supervisor. Score the evidence, not the polish alone. A simple project with strong safety, clear explanation, correct measurements, and complete documentation should score well.

Use the rubric consistently. If a student’s circuit does not fully work, still evaluate process evidence: planning, safety, troubleshooting, documentation, and explanation. Do not reward unsafe shortcuts even if the circuit operates.

Student Demonstration Structure

  1. State the project purpose and approved source.
  2. Identify major components and their roles.
  3. Explain the source-path-load-control-return path.
  4. Demonstrate safe power-up and operation.
  5. Show at least two measurements or verification checks.
  6. Summarize one problem found and how it was corrected.
  7. Submit documentation and answer instructor questions.

Rubric Evidence Table

Instructor Question Bank

  • What is the purpose of your circuit?
  • What source are you using, and why is it approved?
  • Where does current travel when the circuit operates?
  • What component limits or controls current?
  • What test point proves source voltage is present?
  • What fault did you find or plan for?
  • What would you check first if this circuit failed tomorrow?

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may demo without explaining. Pause and ask them to trace the circuit.
  • Students may forget measurement evidence. Require at least two verification points.
  • Students may hide problems. Reward honest troubleshooting documentation.
  • Students may overfocus on appearance. Redirect to function, safety, and evidence.

Documentation Expectations

Each student submits the final project plan, updated schematic or layout, parts list, measurement log, repair ticket or verification ticket, final status summary, and self-reflection. The instructor attaches rubric scoring or signoff.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why is a demonstration more than showing that the circuit turns on?
  2. What evidence should support a final project grade?
  3. How should a student explain a project that only partially works?
  4. Why is honest documentation important in technician work?

Exit Ticket

Students write one technical strength, one documentation strength, and one improvement goal before the final quiz/readiness day.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for demo expectations, 100 minutes for student demonstrations, 25 minutes for documentation/rubric review, and remaining time for coaching, makeups, shutdown, and AAR. Students who need more time should receive a focused revision target rather than rebuilding the whole project.
