Final project build, safe construction sequence, staged testing, measurement logs, and instructor coaching.

CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa readiness in circuit construction, wiring, DMM use, continuity checks, safe source application, measurement documentation, and technician workmanship. Students move from approved plan to controlled build and test.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Require students to build in stages. They should not fully wire a circuit, apply power, and hope. A professional technician builds, inspects, tests continuity, applies power only after approval, measures expected points, and records evidence. Reinforce one change at a time and one measurement at a time.

The instructor should circulate constantly during the first power-up stage. Any project with uncertain wiring, missing current-limiting resistance, reversed polarity, loose connections, or unclear source control should be stopped before energizing.

Expanded Student Explanation

Building is not separate from testing. Each build step should produce something that can be inspected or verified. After placing components, students should compare the physical layout to their plan. Before power is connected, students should check polarity, resistor placement, continuity where appropriate, and source connections. After power is applied, students should measure and record values instead of relying only on appearance.

Build Sequence

  1. Review the signed project plan and parts list.
  2. Lay out components without power connected.
  3. Build the source path, load path, control path, and return path in stages.
  4. Perform visual inspection and peer/instructor review.
  5. Use continuity or resistance checks only where appropriate and de-energized.
  6. Apply approved DC source only after instructor approval.
  7. Record operation, measurements, and any corrections.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may power the circuit before inspection. Stop and reinforce the signoff rule.
  • Students may test only when the circuit fails. Require measurements even when it appears to work.
  • Students may change multiple wires at once. Require one change, one retest, one note.
  • Students may leave messy wiring. Coach them to route wires so faults are visible.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why is staged building safer than wiring everything at once?
  2. What must happen before first power-up?
  3. Why should a working circuit still be measured?
  4. What information belongs in a measurement log?

Exit Ticket

Students submit a build status note: completed sections, measurements taken, problems found, corrections made, and next step for Day 3.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for safety review, 20 minutes for build sequence demonstration, 90 minutes for staged build/test, 25 minutes for documentation review, and remaining time for coaching, shutdown, and AAR. Students who cannot pass first power-up inspection should stop building and correct the plan/layout.
