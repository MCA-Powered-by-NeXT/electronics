Expanded Guide: Week 6 Day 3

Final project troubleshooting, repair-ticket discipline, documentation cleanup, and readiness evidence.

Week 6Student Day PageTroubleshooting Checkoff

CETa Objective Connection

This day reinforces CETa technician work procedures: symptom description, expected operation, safe test planning, measurement evidence, repair documentation, final verification, and professional communication. Students should show that they can troubleshoot without guessing.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Day 3 is the transition from building to proving. Students should identify remaining problems, troubleshoot systematically, and prepare clear documentation for the final demonstration. Encourage students to slow down and document evidence before moving parts or rewiring.

Use the same language from Week 5: symptom, expected operation, hypothesis, test plan, measurement, conclusion, correction, retest. Every correction should have a reason and a verification step.

Expanded Student Explanation

A technician’s job is not finished when the circuit begins to work. The technician must be able to explain what was wrong, what evidence identified the issue, what correction was made, and how the repair was verified. Documentation protects the technician, helps the next person understand the work, and creates evidence for certification readiness.

Guided Troubleshooting Process

  1. State the symptom without guessing the cause.
  2. Restate expected operation from the project plan.
  3. Inspect for obvious safety or workmanship issues with power removed.
  4. Choose one test that can confirm or reject a likely fault.
  5. Record the result before making a correction.
  6. Correct one confirmed issue at a time.
  7. Retest and update the documentation.

Key Vocabulary

Hands-On Practice

Students complete a final project repair ticket even if the project is working. If no active fault remains, students document one issue found earlier or perform a preventive verification ticket that records expected operation, test points, measured evidence, and final status.

Repair Ticket FieldRequired Detail

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may write “fixed wiring” without detail. Require location and evidence.
  • Students may keep changing parts until something works. Stop and require a hypothesis.
  • Students may forget to retest after repair. Require verification before signoff.
  • Students may ignore documentation because the demo is close. Explain that documentation is part of the grade and readiness evidence.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why should a symptom be written before a cause is named?
  2. What makes a hypothesis testable?
  3. Why is retesting required after a correction?
  4. What repair-ticket information would help another technician?

Exit Ticket

Students submit a final project status summary: current project state, one verified measurement, one remaining risk, and what must be ready for the demonstration.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for troubleshooting review, 75 minutes for project troubleshooting, 35 minutes for repair-ticket writing, 25 minutes for instructor conferences, and remaining time for documentation cleanup, shutdown, and AAR. Students with incomplete evidence should focus on documentation before adding more circuit features.
