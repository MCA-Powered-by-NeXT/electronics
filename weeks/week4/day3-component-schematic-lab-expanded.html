CETa Objective Connection

This lab day connects component identification, schematic-symbol recognition, wiring practice, breadboard construction, continuity awareness, and documentation. Students should demonstrate that they can move from a drawing to a safe physical build without guessing.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Treat this as a controlled integration lab. Students are combining earlier skills: safety inspection, component identification, circuit tracing, and meter habits. Require students to complete the plan before the build. Do not let them energize a circuit simply because it “looks close.”

Use the language of technician evidence: planned circuit, inspected components, verified wiring, measured result, documented observation, and corrected fault if needed.

Expanded Student Explanation

A lab is not just a place to try things. It is a structured process for proving that a circuit has been built correctly and safely. Before power is applied, the technician should identify every part, match it to the schematic, confirm the path, inspect the wiring, and predict what should happen.

If the circuit does not behave as expected, the technician does not randomly move parts. The technician returns to the schematic, checks the source, checks polarity, checks row placement, checks component value, and documents each change.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Show the lab schematic and have students identify each component.
  2. Create a build plan on the board: source, resistor, LED/load, return path, and test points.
  3. Place components without power and narrate each placement.
  4. Perform a visual inspection using the schematic.
  5. Ask students to predict the expected behavior.
  6. Apply approved low-voltage DC power only after the class identifies the safety checks.

Hands-On Practice

Students complete the lab in phases. Each phase requires instructor approval before moving forward.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may skip the plan and start wiring. Stop and require a completed schematic annotation.
  • Students may place LED polarity backward. Require polarity identification before insertion.
  • Students may misunderstand breadboard row connections. Demonstrate row continuity again if needed.
  • Students may fix faults without documenting. Require before/after notes.

Documentation Expectations

Students submit an annotated schematic, component table, build checklist, expected result, actual result, and any correction notes. The instructor should initial the plan, pre-power inspection, and final operating result.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why should a schematic be annotated before the physical build?
  2. What must be inspected before power is connected?
  3. Why is a prediction useful before testing?
  4. What should be documented if a circuit does not work at first?

Exit Ticket

Write the five-step lab proof process: plan, build, verify, test, document. Add one sentence explaining which step prevents random troubleshooting.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for review/safety, 30 minutes for demonstration, 80 minutes for phased lab work, 20 minutes for documentation, and remaining time for checkoff, shutdown, and AAR. Students with repeated placement errors should complete a breadboard row-mapping mini-practice before continuing.
