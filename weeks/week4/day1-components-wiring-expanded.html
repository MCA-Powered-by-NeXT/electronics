Component identification, wiring discipline, polarity, terminals, connectors, and beginner workmanship habits.

CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa objectives related to electronic components, wiring, connectors, terminals, polarity, schematic-symbol recognition, workmanship, and technician procedures. Students should be able to identify common parts, describe their purpose, handle them safely, and build clean low-voltage wiring habits.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Week 4 shifts students from basic theory and measurement into component-level recognition and circuit construction. Teach components as physical objects and as symbols. A resistor on the bench, a resistor on a schematic, and a resistor in a circuit all represent the same idea, but students often need repeated practice connecting those views.

Stress wiring discipline: correct row, correct polarity, short leads when possible, no loose strands, no exposed conductor where it should not be exposed, and no power applied until the build has been inspected. Reinforce that neat wiring is not cosmetic; it makes troubleshooting and safety inspection easier.

Expanded Student Explanation

Every component has a job. A resistor limits current. An LED produces light and has polarity. A switch opens or closes a path. A fuse protects a circuit by opening when too much current flows. A potentiometer changes resistance. A terminal or connector provides a controlled place to join conductors. A wire is not just a piece of metal; it is a planned path that must be routed, secured, and documented.

Students should learn to inspect components before using them. Bent leads, damaged insulation, cracked housings, burned marks, loose terminals, and unclear markings all matter. A technician does not guess that a part is good; a technician identifies it, inspects it, uses the correct tool, and verifies the result.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Display each major component: resistor, LED, switch, fuse, potentiometer, wire, terminal, and connector.
  2. Have students identify physical markings and compare them to schematic symbols.
  3. Show good and poor wire stripping examples.
  4. Demonstrate how excess exposed conductor can create accidental contact.
  5. Build a simple source-resistor-LED path without power, then inspect it aloud.
  6. Only after inspection, apply approved low-voltage DC power and observe operation.

Hands-On Practice

Students complete a component identification and wiring preparation station. They identify each component, state its purpose, list whether polarity matters, draw its schematic symbol if known, and inspect a sample wire or connector for safe use.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may identify components by color or shape only. Require name, purpose, and symbol connection.
  • Students may over-strip wires. Show the difference between enough conductor and unsafe exposed conductor.
  • Students may ignore LED polarity. Require anode/cathode identification before inserting into a circuit.
  • Students may treat neatness as optional. Connect neat routing to easier troubleshooting and safer inspection.

Documentation Expectations

Students produce a component table, wiring inspection notes, and one corrected workmanship example. They should document not only what they used, but why the component was appropriate and what inspection proved it was safe to use.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why is component identification more than naming a part?
  2. What does polarity mean, and why does it matter for LEDs?
  3. How can poor wire stripping create a fault?
  4. Why does clean wiring make troubleshooting easier?

Exit Ticket

Choose one component and write: its name, function, schematic symbol description, one inspection point, and one common mistake to avoid.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 30 minutes for component introduction, 25 minutes for symbol matching, 25 minutes for wiring/workmanship demo, 50 minutes for station practice, 20 minutes for documentation, and the remainder for checkoff/shutdown/AAR. Students who cannot connect physical parts to schematic symbols should repeat the sort-and-match activity before Day 2.
