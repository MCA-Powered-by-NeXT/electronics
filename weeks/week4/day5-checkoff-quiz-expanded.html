CETa Objective Connection

This milestone verifies Week 4 readiness in component identification, schematic tracing, wiring/workmanship, breadboard construction, series/parallel recognition, documentation, and safe lab shutdown. No new instruction should be introduced.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Run this day as a milestone station day. Students rotate through review, practical checkoff, quiz, makeups, and coaching. The goal is to collect evidence and identify remediation needs before moving into Week 5 troubleshooting.

Use the checkoff as a performance observation, not a lecture. If a student cannot explain a component, trace a circuit, or justify a wiring choice, move them to coaching and reattempt rather than marking them ready too soon.

Expanded Student Explanation

A milestone day is a proof day. Students show what they can do without the instructor reteaching the entire week. The evidence should include a completed quiz, a practical checkoff, corrected documentation if needed, and an AAR statement about personal readiness.

Technicians are evaluated by evidence. If the circuit is neat, the schematic is annotated, the component choices are correct, and the student can explain the path, the work is defensible. If the circuit only works by accident, the student is not ready for troubleshooting.

Checkoff Criteria

  • [ ] Identifies resistor, LED, switch, fuse, wire, connector/terminal, and potentiometer if available.
  • [ ] Explains at least three component functions.
  • [ ] Identifies polarity-sensitive components.
  • [ ] Traces a simple schematic from source through load and return.
  • [ ] Distinguishes series and parallel paths using evidence.
  • [ ] Builds or inspects a breadboard circuit with power removed.
  • [ ] Documents fault or correction notes clearly.
  • [ ] Completes shutdown and tool return.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may rely on memory instead of tracing. Require finger/marker tracing on the schematic.
  • Students may pass a working circuit without documentation. Require evidence before signoff.
  • Students may confuse component value and component function. Ask both questions separately.
  • Students may need remediation on breadboard row connections. Use a row-continuity mini-check.

Quiz Readiness Review

Review only the concepts already taught: component purpose, schematic symbols, nodes, polarity, wiring safety, series/parallel path behavior, and basic documentation. Do not introduce new circuit types on the quiz day.

AAR Prompts

  1. What component or symbol do you understand best now?
  2. What part of schematic tracing still slows you down?
  3. What mistake did you correct this week?
  4. What evidence proves you are ready for Week 5 troubleshooting?

Exit Ticket

Students write one sentence each for: component readiness, schematic readiness, breadboard readiness, and one personal improvement target for Week 5.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 10 minutes for check-in, 10 minutes for safety brief, 30 minutes for review station, 60 minutes for practical checkoffs, 35 minutes for quiz, 30 minutes for coaching/makeups, 10 minutes for shutdown, and 5 minutes for AAR. Students not ready should receive a specific remediation target before troubleshooting week begins.
