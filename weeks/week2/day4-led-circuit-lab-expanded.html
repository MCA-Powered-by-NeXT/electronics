CETa Objective Connection

This lab supports CETa objectives in circuit construction, component identification, wiring, approved DC sources, resistance, polarity, safety precautions, and technician documentation. Students convert Week 2 theory into observable hands-on performance.

Instructor Teaching Notes

This is the week’s main hands-on build. Students should not simply copy a circuit. They should explain the source, current path, LED polarity, resistor purpose, and expected behavior before energizing. The instructor should require a pre-power inspection before each student applies power.

Use the lab to reinforce professional pacing. Students may want to rush because lighting an LED feels simple. Slow them down: identify parts, decode resistor, sketch path, inspect breadboard rows, connect power last, observe, disconnect, document.

Expanded Student Explanation

An LED is a directional component. It must be oriented correctly for current to pass through it. The resistor limits current and protects the LED. The breadboard creates hidden connection paths, so students must understand which holes are connected before assuming the circuit is correct.

A successful LED circuit is not just an LED that turns on. A successful technician build is safe, explainable, documented, and repeatable. If another student or instructor cannot follow your drawing and notes, the work is not complete.

Instructor Demonstration Sequence

  1. Show the LED and identify anode/cathode indicators.
  2. Show the resistor and verify its value.
  3. Draw the complete circuit path on the board.
  4. Map the drawing to breadboard rows.
  5. Build the circuit with power disconnected.
  6. Perform pre-power inspection aloud.
  7. Connect the approved source and observe.
  8. Disconnect power before making any change.

Lab Procedure

  1. Complete PPE and bench inspection.
  2. Identify LED polarity and resistor value.
  3. Draw the intended circuit path.
  4. Have the instructor approve the drawing.
  5. Build with power disconnected.
  6. Request pre-power inspection.
  7. Apply power only after approval.
  8. Observe LED behavior.
  9. Disconnect power before adjustment.
  10. Record results and corrective actions.

Safety Boundaries

  • Only approved low-voltage DC sources are used.
  • No live AC sources or measurements are allowed.
  • Power is connected last and disconnected before any change.
  • Students may not bypass the resistor.
  • Any heat, odor, smoke, or battery concern requires immediate stop and instructor notification.

Documentation Evidence

Students submit a circuit sketch, part identification, pre-power checklist, observation table, and short troubleshooting note if the circuit did not work on the first attempt. The instructor signs off only after the student can explain the source, path, load, resistor purpose, and LED polarity.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why does LED polarity matter?
  2. Why must the resistor be included?
  3. Why is the breadboard row pattern important?
  4. What should you do before changing a wire?
  5. What evidence proves your circuit works safely?

Exit Ticket

Write a four-sentence technician report: what you built, what parts you used, what happened when powered, and what you verified before shutdown.

Instructor Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for safety and part review, 20 minutes for demonstration, 30 minutes for drawing/prep, 55 minutes for supervised build, 25 minutes for troubleshooting and documentation, and remaining time for checkoff, shutdown, and AAR. Students who cannot explain the path should trace it with a finger on the unpowered breadboard before rebuilding.
