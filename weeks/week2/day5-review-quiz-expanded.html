CETa Objective Connection

This milestone day reinforces CETa electrical theory, formulas, component identification, approved DC source boundaries, circuit construction, documentation, and technician work procedures. No new instruction should occur; the day is for review, assessment, coaching, makeups, and evidence completion.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Use this milestone to find misunderstandings before Week 3 DMM work begins. Students who cannot explain voltage, current, resistance, resistor purpose, or LED polarity will struggle when measurements are added. Review should be active: students calculate, identify, draw, explain, and correct—not simply listen.

Separate the class into groups if needed: quiz-ready students complete CETa practice and documentation checks, while students who need remediation repeat resistor ID, circuit-path tracing, or Ohm's Law calculation practice.

Quiz Readiness Targets

  • Define voltage, current, and resistance correctly.
  • Identify source, path, load, open circuit, and short circuit.
  • Decode basic resistor values using a chart.
  • Explain tolerance and measurement verification.
  • Use Ohm's Law with units.
  • Explain why current limiting protects an LED.
  • Describe approved DC source boundaries and no-live-AC policy.
  • Document a build or fault using clear technician language.

Instructor-Led Review Prompts

  1. A battery is connected to nothing. Does it have voltage? Is current flowing? Explain.
  2. An LED does not light. Name five possible causes before touching the circuit.
  3. A resistor reads 980 ohms but the color code says 1,000 ohms with 5 percent tolerance. Is it acceptable?
  4. What is unsafe about changing breadboard wires while power is connected?
  5. Why should a technician calculate expected current before powering a circuit?

Makeup and Remediation Plan

Students missing Day 1–4 evidence complete the most important evidence first: safety brief, resistor identification table, Ohm's Law prediction sheet, LED circuit sketch, and lab observation note. The instructor should not accept incomplete evidence as a quiz substitute; the goal is to document both knowledge and performance.

Common Misunderstandings to Correct

  • Voltage flows. Correction: current flows; voltage is measured between points.
  • Resistor color code must be memorized perfectly. Correction: technicians may use charts but must verify correctly.
  • An LED is just a light bulb. Correction: LED polarity and current limits matter.
  • Any DC source is acceptable. Correction: only instructor-approved low-voltage training sources may be used.
  • If the LED lights, the work is done. Correction: the circuit must be explainable and documented.

Assessment Evidence

Collect quiz score, station sheets, missing lab evidence, and AAR notes. Students who do not meet readiness should receive a remediation task before Week 3 measurement labs.

Exit Ticket

Students answer: “What is one concept from Week 2 that you can now explain clearly, and what is one concept you still need to practice before using a DMM?”

Instructor Pacing and Remediation

Use 15 minutes for check-in/safety, 25 minutes for whole-class review, 55 minutes for stations, 30 minutes for quiz, 25 minutes for makeup/coaching, 15 minutes for documentation collection, and final time for shutdown and AAR. Remediate before moving students into independent measurement work.
