CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa component-identification and electrical-theory objectives. Students learn how resistors control current, divide voltage, protect LEDs, and provide predictable circuit behavior. The color code is treated as a technician identification skill, not just a memorization exercise.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Begin with the practical reason resistors matter: without resistance in the correct place, beginner LED circuits may draw too much current. Students should understand that resistor value, tolerance, and placement affect circuit behavior. Emphasize that technicians verify component values instead of relying only on memory or color bands.

Use large printed resistor-color-code charts and real resistors. Some students will struggle with small bands, lighting, color perception, or band direction. Give permission to use a chart and a meter. The professional goal is correct identification and verification, not guessing quickly.

Expanded Student Explanation

A resistor is a component that opposes current flow. In beginner circuits, resistors are commonly used to protect LEDs, create predictable current levels, and help technicians control circuit behavior. Resistance is measured in ohms. Larger resistance usually means less current for the same voltage.

The resistor color code is a compact way of printing a value on a small part. The first bands give significant digits, the multiplier tells how many zeros to add, and the tolerance band tells how close the actual resistor may be to the marked value. A resistor labeled 220 ohms with 5 percent tolerance may not measure exactly 220 ohms, but it should be close enough for the intended circuit.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Show several resistors with different values and sizes.
  2. Explain that physical size may relate to power rating, not simply resistance value.
  3. Use a chart to decode one resistor slowly.
  4. Decode the same resistor again using student volunteers.
  5. Measure the resistor with a DMM and compare measured value to tolerance.
  6. Place the resistor in an LED current-limiting example and explain its purpose.

Guided Practice

Students decode at least five resistors and record color bands, calculated value, tolerance, measured value, and whether the resistor passes tolerance expectation.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may read the tolerance band first. Teach them to identify the separated gold/silver/tolerance band.
  • Students may confuse red/orange or brown/red. Improve lighting and allow chart use.
  • Students may forget the multiplier. Require them to say the value aloud before writing it.
  • Students may assume exact measurement is required. Explain tolerance and normal variation.
  • Students may insert the resistor in the wrong breadboard row later. Connect identification to placement and circuit path.

Safety and Quality Boundaries

Students should not energize an LED circuit until the current-limiting resistor has been identified, placed correctly, and instructor-approved. The resistor-color-code activity is low risk, but the circuit that uses the resistor still requires power control, polarity check, and bench organization.

Documentation Evidence

Students submit a resistor identification table with at least five parts. The instructor checks that students used both color-code reading and measurement verification. This table becomes evidence for component identification and documentation.

Check for Understanding

  1. What does a resistor do in an LED circuit?
  2. What does tolerance mean?
  3. Why should a technician measure a resistor after reading the bands?
  4. How can reading the bands backward cause a circuit problem?
  5. Why is a resistor value important before applying power?

Exit Ticket

Decode one assigned resistor, measure it, and write one sentence explaining whether the measurement agrees with the color-code value and tolerance.

Instructor Pacing and Remediation

Plan 20 minutes for resistor purpose, 25 minutes for color-code demonstration, 45 minutes for identification table practice, 20 minutes for DMM verification, 20 minutes for LED-current-limiting connection, and remaining time for review/checkoff/shutdown. Students who struggle should use larger visual resistor cards before returning to physical parts.
