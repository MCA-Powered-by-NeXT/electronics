Expanded Guide: Week 3 Day 4

DMM practical checkoff preparation, measurement stations, safety verification, and remediation coaching.

Week 3Student Day PageDMM Checkoff

CETa Objective Connection

This day verifies CETa readiness for safe DMM handling, DC voltage measurement, resistance measurement, continuity testing, meter setup, lead placement, documentation, and technician decision-making.

Instructor Teaching Notes

This is not a speed test. It is a performance-evidence day. Students must demonstrate that they can set up the meter safely, explain their measurement plan, take a reading, interpret the reading, and document the result. The instructor should grade observable habits, not just correct numbers.

Keep stations low-voltage and de-energized where appropriate. Do not allow students to troubleshoot by random probing. Each station begins with a plan and ends with documentation.

Checkoff Structure

StationSkillPassing Evidence
1Meter inspection and setupCorrect function, lead placement, test points, and power-state statement.
2DC voltage measurementSafe across-point measurement with correct units and polarity awareness.
3Resistance measurementDe-energized/isolated measurement with expected versus actual value.
4Continuity testCorrect open/closed path interpretation and documentation.
5Unsafe setup identificationStudent stops, explains the fault, and corrects setup before proceeding.

Instructor Demonstration Sequence

  1. Explain the checkoff standard and remind students that unsafe setup stops the attempt.
  2. Model one station from start to finish using the required verbal plan.
  3. Show a sample passing log entry and a sample incomplete log entry.
  4. Review remediation process: pause, coach, retry with a different station if needed.

Student Performance Script

Require students to say a version of this before each measurement: “I am measuring _____. My meter is set to _____. My black lead is in COM and my red lead is in _____. The circuit is _____. My probes will touch _____ and _____. I expect approximately _____.”

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may rush and skip setup language. Require a restart of the station.
  • Students may forget units. Mark documentation incomplete until corrected.
  • Students may try resistance/continuity on a powered circuit. Stop immediately and remediate.
  • Students may misinterpret OL/open readings. Use a known-open example to reteach.

Documentation Expectations

Each station requires a log entry with station number, task, meter setting, jack placement, circuit state, expected result, actual result, units, conclusion, and instructor initials. Students should keep the completed sheet as assessment evidence.

Readiness Decisions

ResultMeaningInstructor Action
PassStudent demonstrates safe independent setup and accurate documentation.Sign off and move to enrichment.
Pass with coachingMinor documentation or confidence issue, no unsafe behavior.Assign targeted practice and recheck.
Not yetUnsafe setup, repeated incorrect mode/jack choice, or missing power-state awareness.Stop assessment, reteach, and reschedule checkoff.

Exit Ticket

Students write one safety rule they will always follow before using a DMM and one measurement habit they improved during the checkoff.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for review, 15 minutes for demonstration, 90 minutes for station checkoffs, 20 minutes for remediation, and the rest for documentation, shutdown, and AAR. Students who finish early can assist with setup cleanup, complete extra measurement logs, or practice reading tolerance values.
