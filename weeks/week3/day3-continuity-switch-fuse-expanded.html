CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa objectives related to wiring, switches, fuses, connectors, continuity, open circuits, and basic troubleshooting. Students learn to test whether a path exists and to describe the meaning of continuity readings without guessing.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Continuity is one of the most useful beginner troubleshooting tests, but it is often misunderstood. Teach continuity as evidence of a conductive path, not as proof that a full circuit will operate correctly. A path may have continuity but still be connected incorrectly, rated incorrectly, or unsafe for the intended load.

Repeat the rule: continuity testing is done on de-energized circuits only. Require students to state how power was removed and verified before testing.

Expanded Student Explanation

A continuity test checks whether electricity has a path from one point to another. When a switch is closed, continuity should normally exist across the switch terminals. When a switch is open, continuity should normally be absent. A good fuse should show continuity because it is designed to pass current. A blown fuse should show open because the internal path has broken.

Technicians use continuity tests to find open wires, broken switches, blown fuses, and missing connections. The test does not tell the whole story by itself. It must be combined with circuit understanding, visual inspection, documentation, and safe power control.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Show a switch and explain open versus closed mechanically and electrically.
  2. Verify power removed before continuity mode.
  3. Test switch continuity in both positions.
  4. Test a known good fuse and a known open/blown fuse or simulated open link.
  5. Test a jumper wire and a damaged/open jumper.
  6. Model documentation: “Switch closed: continuity present; switch open: no continuity; switch behavior normal.”

Hands-On Practice

Students rotate through continuity stations and complete a path-status table. They must identify the component, expected condition, actual meter response, and conclusion.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may test continuity with power connected. Stop and require power-state verification.
  • Students may believe beep equals “good.” Teach them to interpret the beep in context.
  • Students may test the wrong two points. Require them to trace the intended path first.
  • Students may replace a fuse without asking why it opened. Reinforce root-cause thinking.

Documentation Expectations

Continuity documentation must include component name, circuit power state, probe points, expected result, actual result, and conclusion. For failed items, students must write an action such as “remove from service,” “replace fuse after fault check,” or “rebuild wiring path.”

Check for Understanding

  1. Why must continuity tests be done with power removed?
  2. What should a good fuse show on a continuity test?
  3. Why does continuity not always prove the circuit is correct?
  4. How should a technician document a failed jumper wire?

Exit Ticket

Explain the expected continuity result for a switch in the open position, a switch in the closed position, a good fuse, and a broken wire.

Pacing and Remediation

Spend 20 minutes on concept review, 25 minutes on switch/fuse demonstration, 70 minutes on station work, 25 minutes on documentation review, and the remainder on checkoff/shutdown/AAR. Students who over-rely on the continuity beep should repeat a path-tracing task without the meter first.
