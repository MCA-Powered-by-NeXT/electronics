CETa Objective Connection

This milestone consolidates CETa test-equipment readiness. Students review DMM setup, DC voltage, resistance, continuity, current-mode awareness, safe troubleshooting behavior, and professional documentation.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Milestone day should not introduce new content. Use it to identify gaps, reteach weak skills, complete makeups, document checkoffs, and coach students toward the next week. Students who passed the DMM checkoff should still participate in review and help strengthen documentation quality.

Review Topics

  • Meter inspection and lead condition.
  • Correct jack placement for voltage/resistance/continuity.
  • Difference between powered voltage measurement and de-energized resistance/continuity tests.
  • Expected versus actual readings.
  • Units and measurement documentation.
  • Why current mode is high-risk for beginners and not used casually.

Guided Review Questions

  1. A student wants to measure resistance in a powered LED circuit. What should happen before the meter touches the circuit?
  2. The red lead is in the current jack, but the student wants to measure voltage. What is the hazard?
  3. A switch shows continuity in both ON and OFF positions. What could that mean?
  4. A battery reads negative voltage. What should the student check first?
  5. A resistor measures far outside its expected range. What should be checked before declaring it bad?

Quiz Readiness Check

Before the quiz, students should be able to answer the following without notes: what a DMM does, how to measure DC voltage, why resistance requires power removed, what continuity means, why jack placement matters, and what documentation must include.

Common Remediation Needs

  • Voltage/current confusion: return to source/path/load review.
  • Resistance/continuity power-state mistakes: repeat de-energized setup drill.
  • Weak documentation: require complete sample log corrections.
  • Probe placement errors: use printed circuit diagrams and mark test points before physical probing.

Exit Ticket

Students identify one DMM skill they can perform independently, one skill they still need to practice, and one safety rule they will carry into Week 4 component and schematic work.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for safety/check-in, 35 minutes for guided review, 55 minutes for hands-on stations, 25 minutes for quiz, 25 minutes for corrections/remediation, and remaining time for inventory, shutdown, and AAR. Do not move an unsafe DMM user into unsupervised component work until remediation is complete.
