CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa test-equipment and measurement objectives. Students must identify DMM parts, select correct functions, place leads correctly, inspect meter condition, and explain why meter setup is a safety issue before any measurement is attempted.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Teach the DMM as a precision tool, not just a display that produces numbers. The most important habit today is setup-before-contact: inspect the meter, inspect leads, choose the correct function, choose the correct jack, identify what two points are being measured, then touch probes only after the instructor-approved setup is correct.

Emphasize that most beginner meter mistakes happen before the probes touch the circuit. Wrong jack, wrong mode, damaged lead, uncertain circuit state, and current-mode misuse can damage equipment or create unsafe conditions.

Expanded Student Explanation

A digital multimeter can measure several different electrical conditions, but it must be configured for the task. Voltage measurement is done across two points. Resistance and continuity measurements are done only on de-energized circuits or isolated components. Current measurement is different because the meter becomes part of the circuit path; that topic is treated as awareness only unless the instructor specifically authorizes a controlled demonstration.

Students should learn to say the measurement plan aloud: “I am measuring DC voltage across the battery terminals with the black lead in COM, red lead in the voltage jack, meter set to DC volts, and probes touching positive and negative terminals.” This language builds safe habits and helps instructors catch mistakes early.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Show meter body, display, dial/buttons, COM jack, voltage/resistance jack, current jack, leads, and probes.
  2. Demonstrate a pre-use inspection: cracked case, damaged insulation, loose probe tips, missing ratings, and weak battery indication.
  3. Set up for DC voltage and verbalize each step.
  4. Set up for resistance/continuity and verbalize why the circuit must be de-energized.
  5. Point out current jack and explain why it is not used casually.
  6. Have students perform a dry setup with no circuit connected.

Hands-On Practice

Students complete a DMM setup station rotation. At each station, students identify the intended task, choose the function, place leads in the correct jacks, and state whether the circuit must be powered or de-energized. No measurement is taken until the instructor signs off the setup.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may leave the red lead in the current jack. Require lead check before every task.
  • Students may confuse AC and DC symbols. Make them point to the correct symbol before measuring.
  • Students may measure resistance on a powered circuit. Stop the task and require de-energized verification language.
  • Students may chase numbers without knowing test points. Require them to name both points before probing.

Documentation Expectations

Each student records meter model, inspected lead condition, selected mode, lead jack placement, circuit power state, measurement target, and instructor signoff. This creates evidence for DMM safety habits before practical measurement begins.

Check for Understanding

  1. Why must the meter be set before the probes touch the circuit?
  2. What is the difference between voltage measurement and resistance measurement setup?
  3. Why is current mode treated with extra caution?
  4. What should a student check if the meter displays an unexpected reading?

Exit Ticket

Write the four-part meter safety check: function, jack, circuit state, and test points. Add one example of a mistake this check would prevent.

Pacing and Remediation

Spend 25 minutes on meter anatomy, 20 minutes on safety inspection, 30 minutes on setup demonstrations, 60 minutes on station rotations, 15 minutes on documentation, and the remainder on checkoff, shutdown, and AAR. Students who miss jack placement or powered-resistance rules should repeat station practice before moving to Day 2.
