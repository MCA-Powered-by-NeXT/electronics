Expanded Guide: Week 3 Day 2

Accurate DC voltage and resistance measurements using safe DMM setup and documentation habits.

Week 3Student Day PagePrevious Expanded Guide

CETa Objective Connection

This day supports CETa test-equipment objectives for measuring voltage and resistance, recognizing powered versus de-energized tests, documenting readings, and using measurement evidence to confirm circuit condition.

Instructor Teaching Notes

Keep the focus on measurement purpose, not just meter operation. Students should know why they are measuring, what result is expected, what result would be abnormal, and what action is allowed after the reading. Require a prediction before measurement so students learn to judge whether a reading makes sense.

Voltage readings are taken across two points in a powered low-voltage circuit. Resistance readings are taken only on isolated/de-energized components or paths. Make this contrast explicit throughout the class.

Expanded Student Explanation

Voltage measurement answers the question, “How much electrical potential difference exists between these two points?” Resistance measurement answers, “How much does this component or path oppose current when power is removed?” These are different questions, so they require different meter settings and different circuit conditions.

A good technician does not simply write “3 volts.” A good technician records where the probes were placed, what the meter was set to, what the expected value was, what the actual value was, and whether the result was acceptable.

Demonstration Sequence

  1. Review DMM safety setup from Day 1.
  2. Measure a battery voltage while naming the two test points.
  3. Reverse probes intentionally on a DC source and explain polarity indication.
  4. Remove power and measure a resistor value.
  5. Compare expected resistor value to measured value.
  6. Model a complete measurement log entry.

Hands-On Practice

Students complete a measurement log with at least three DC voltage readings and three resistance readings. Each entry must include expected value, meter mode, circuit power state, probe points, actual reading, and technician conclusion.

Common Mistakes and Coaching

  • Students may measure voltage from one point only. Reinforce that voltage is between two points.
  • Students may measure resistance in a connected powered circuit. Require power-state verification before ohms mode.
  • Students may record numbers without units. Require V, Ω, kΩ, or MΩ labels.
  • Students may trust a surprising reading. Teach them to re-check setup before assuming the circuit is wrong.

Documentation Expectations

Measurement logs must be neat enough for another technician to understand. Students should include units, test points, circuit state, expected value, actual value, and a short conclusion such as “within expected range,” “open path suspected,” or “setup rechecked due to unexpected value.”

Check for Understanding

  1. Why is voltage measured across two points?
  2. Why must power be removed for resistance measurements?
  3. What should you do if a measurement does not match your prediction?
  4. Why are units required in a technician log?

Exit Ticket

Write one correct voltage-measurement sentence and one correct resistance-measurement sentence. Each sentence must include meter mode, power state, probe points, and expected result.

Pacing and Remediation

Use 20 minutes for safety review, 30 minutes for demonstration, 75 minutes for measurement stations, 25 minutes for log review, and the rest for checkoff/shutdown/AAR. Students who miss power-state rules should repeat a setup-only station before touching a circuit again.
